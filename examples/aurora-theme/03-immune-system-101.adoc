== Meet the Cells

Neutrophils:: First responders; engulf and destroy pathogens within minutes.
Macrophages:: Larger scavenger cells; clean up debris and recruit reinforcements.
Dendritic cells:: Messengers that carry pathogen fragments to the nearest lymph node.
B cells:: Produce antibodies tailored to one specific pathogen.
T cells:: Coordinate the response and destroy infected cells directly.

=== Macrophages, Closer Up

A macrophage is part scavenger, part alarm system.
It swallows debris and dead cells, then displays fragments of whatever it found on its own surface — a signal that recruits the adaptive immune system if the threat looks unfamiliar.

=== T Cells, Closer Up

Helper T cells coordinate — they release signals that activate B cells and other immune cells.
Killer T cells execute — they find infected cells displaying suspicious fragments and destroy them directly.

== How a Response Unfolds

[%step]
. A pathogen breaches skin or mucosa
. Innate cells (neutrophils, macrophages) respond within minutes
. Dendritic cells carry pathogen fragments to a lymph node
. B and T cells matching that fragment multiply rapidly
. Antibodies neutralize the pathogen; killer T cells clear infected cells
. Memory B and T cells persist, ready for next time

== Check Your Understanding

[%collapsible]
.Why don't we get sick from a flu vaccine itself?
====
Most flu vaccines contain inactivated virus or just a viral protein — there's no live virus able to replicate and cause illness.
Soreness or a mild fever is your immune system responding to training, not to infection.
====

[%collapsible]
.Can your immune system "remember" too much and overreact?
====
Yes — that overreaction is roughly what happens in autoimmune conditions and severe allergies: the adaptive immune system responds to something that isn't actually a threat.
====

[%collapsible]
.Why do some vaccines need a booster and others don't?
====
It depends on how strong and how long-lived the memory response is to that specific antigen.
Some pathogens produce very durable memory cells after one exposure; others fade faster or mutate enough that the original memory stops matching well.
====

== Common Misconceptions

[NOTE]
Antibiotics do nothing against viruses — they target bacterial machinery that viruses simply don't have.

[WARNING]
"Boosting" your immune system isn't always good: an overactive response is what causes much of the damage in severe infections.

[TIP]
A fever isn't the illness — it's your body raising the temperature to make it harder for many pathogens to replicate.
