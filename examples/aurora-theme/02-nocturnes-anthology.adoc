== A Note on This Collection

Six short pieces, gathered over three years of insomnia and one very slow train.
Read them the way you'd read a room at midnight — slowly, and without turning on every light.

[.violet.background]
== I. Night Walks

== Night Walks

[verse, Elena Voss, Nocturnes, 2019]
____
The city exhales its neon breath,
and I follow the wet pavement home.
Every window a small yellow sun
that isn't mine to keep warm.
____

== On Translation

Translation carries loss and invention in equal measure.footnote:[Translations by E. Voss, first published in the 2019 chapbook alongside the originals.]
Here is the same stanza, twice — press the space bar to flip between them.

[.r-stack]
--
[verse.fade-in-then-out%step, Aurélie Bonnet, Marée]
____
La lune tire la mer par la manche,
et la mer, docile, la suit.
Nous aussi, nous partons sans bruit
quand quelque chose nous appelle.
____

[verse%step, Aurélie Bonnet, "Tide (transl. E. Voss)"]
____
The moon tugs the sea by its sleeve,
and the sea, obedient, follows.
We too leave without a sound
when something calls us on.
____

== II. Letters

== Letters Never Sent

[verse, Elena Voss, Nocturnes, 2019]
____
I write you every night I don't call,
fold the pages into a drawer
that is slowly becoming a person
made entirely of things unsaid.
____

== On Reading in the Dark

[quote, Tomas Rehn, On Reading in the Dark (2020)]
____
We read at night not to see more clearly, but to let the sentence become the only light in the room.
____

[.gold.background]
== III. Coda

== Coda

[verse, Elena Voss, Nocturnes, 2019]
____
Not every light needs to arrive somewhere.
Some of them just want to be seen
leaving.
____
